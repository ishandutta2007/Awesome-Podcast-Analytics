Top Podcast Analytics Ecosystem

Curated List of SaaS Products & Open-Source GitHub Projects
Focused on Podcast Downloads, Audience Measurement, Listener Analytics, Attribution, Distribution Intelligence, Advertising Analytics & Podcast Performance
Last updated: September 2026

This repository tracks notable SaaS/hosted platforms and open-source projects for Podcast Analytics. These tools help podcasters, podcast networks, publishers, advertisers, agencies, and hosting providers measure downloads, unique listeners, audience growth, listening platforms, geographic distribution, episode performance, attribution, advertising campaigns, and engagement.

Typical capabilities include download measurement, IAB-style audience analytics, unique listener estimation, listener geography, podcast app and device statistics, episode-level performance, RSS analytics, prefix analytics, advertising attribution, campaign measurement, platform comparison, trend analysis, data exports, and self-hosted analytics dashboards.

Examples include Chartable, Podsights, Podtrac, Spotify for Creators Analytics, Transistor Analytics, Simplecast Analytics, Libsyn Stats, Blubrry Statistics, OP3, and Backtracks.

Open-source emphasis: This section is heavily expanded with major open-source podcast analytics platforms, self-hosted podcast hosts with built-in analytics, open analytics prefixes, open data pipelines, RSS analytics tools, web analytics platforms, event analytics systems, dashboards, databases, and data-processing frameworks. The fully integrated open-source podcast analytics market is smaller than the commercial market, but a strong modular ecosystem exists for building transparent, privacy-focused, and self-hosted podcast measurement systems.

Open-Source GitHub Projects

OP3 — Open Podcast Prefix Project
One of the most important open-source podcast analytics projects. OP3 provides a privacy-focused podcast analytics prefix, open data, public statistics, API access, and auditable download calculations for podcast downloads.

Open Podcast Stack
Self-hosted open podcast analytics stack combining an API, RSS request forwarder, Spotify and Apple connectors, automation components, MySQL, and Metabase for building a standalone podcast analytics environment.

Open Podcast Pipelines
Open-source podcast analytics data pipelines for collecting and processing podcast metrics from services such as Spotify, Apple, Podigee, and Anchor-compatible sources and forwarding the data into an Open Podcast API.

Open Podcast Ecosystem
Open-source ecosystem containing an analytics API, analytics frontend, data pipelines, self-hosted stack, podcast RSS forwarder, Spotify connector, Apple connector, browser integrations, and open podcast analytics standards.

Castopod
Full-featured open-source and self-hosted podcast hosting platform with built-in analytics, including downloads, unique listeners, listening time, podcast applications, devices, operating systems, geographic distribution, and listener time patterns.

OP3 MCP
Open-source MCP server that exposes OP3 podcast analytics to AI assistants and agents, including download trends, listener geography, podcast application share, and episode-level analytics.

Podcast Analytics Dashboard
Open-source dashboard project for tracking podcast downloads and streams across Spotify, Apple Podcasts, and YouTube, with episode analytics, trend reporting, platform comparisons, performance insights, and CSV exports.

Matomo
Major open-source and self-hosted web analytics platform that can be used to measure podcast website visits, landing-page performance, referrals, campaign traffic, listener acquisition, and conversion funnels.

Additional Strong Open-Source Options

Podcast-specific analytics: OP3 is one of the strongest direct open-source projects for podcast download measurement, using an analytics prefix and open, auditable infrastructure. Open Podcast provides a complementary self-hosted ecosystem for collecting and consolidating analytics data from multiple podcast platforms.

Self-hosted podcast platforms: Castopod is especially notable because it combines podcast hosting with built-in analytics, including downloads, unique listeners, listening time, devices, applications, operating systems, locations, and listening patterns.

Podcast request analytics: OP3, GoAccess, AWStats, OpenObserve, ClickHouse, and custom log-processing pipelines can provide transparent analysis of podcast download requests.

Web and audience acquisition analytics: Matomo, Plausible, Umami, Ackee, Shynet, and Open Web Analytics can measure podcast websites, episode pages, referral traffic, campaigns, and listener acquisition funnels.

Product and event analytics: PostHog and Countly can track engagement within custom podcast websites and applications, including player interactions, subscriptions, account creation, episode discovery, and audience journeys.

Analytics dashboards: Apache Superset, Metabase, and Grafana can provide self-hosted podcast reporting for publishers, networks, advertisers, and podcast production teams.

Real-time analytics: Apache Kafka, Apache Flink, ClickHouse, TimescaleDB, Prometheus, and Grafana can form a scalable real-time podcast analytics infrastructure.

Large-scale log analysis: Apache Spark, DuckDB, ClickHouse, OpenSearch, and PostgreSQL can process large historical collections of podcast downloads and audience events.

Podcast data pipelines: Apache Airflow, Prefect, Apache NiFi, dbt Core, and Great Expectations can automate ingestion, transformation, validation, and reporting of podcast analytics data.

AI and predictive analytics: pandas, Polars, Jupyter, scikit-learn, MLflow, and open-source language-model frameworks can be used for episode performance forecasting, audience segmentation, anomaly detection, and automated analytics reporting.

Advertising analytics: Open-source event pipelines and analytical databases can be combined with campaign identifiers, referral data, and conversion events to build custom podcast advertising attribution systems.

Privacy-focused measurement: OP3's approach is particularly relevant for transparent podcast analytics because its source code and data-processing approach are publicly auditable while raw listener IP addresses are not exposed through its public data model.

Many community projects exist for RSS analytics, podcast dashboards, download log processing, Spotify and Apple metrics collection, podcast marketing analytics, audio-player event tracking, and audience intelligence.

Frameworks for building custom systems: Combine OP3 as an open podcast download analytics layer with Castopod for self-hosted podcast hosting and built-in audience statistics. Use the Open Podcast Stack and Open Podcast Pipelines to consolidate metrics from multiple podcast platforms into a self-hosted analytics environment.

Store raw download and event data in PostgreSQL, ClickHouse, TimescaleDB, or DuckDB, process it using Apache Kafka, Apache Flink, Apache Spark, pandas, or Polars, and automate workflows with Apache Airflow, Prefect, or Apache NiFi.

Build reporting with Metabase, Apache Superset, and Grafana, while using Matomo, Plausible, Umami, or PostHog to measure podcast website traffic and listener acquisition.

This modular architecture can provide a transparent, privacy-conscious, and self-hosted alternative for podcast analytics. Commercial platforms may still offer more mature proprietary integrations, advertising attribution networks, IAB-certified measurement services, cross-platform identity resolution, and managed publisher reporting.

Disclaimer

This is a community-curated list — not exhaustive and not an endorsement.

Podcast download metrics, listens, streams, unique listeners, and audience estimates are not always directly comparable across hosting providers and analytics systems.

A podcast download does not necessarily represent a complete listen or an individual unique listener.

Different analytics platforms may use different filtering, attribution, bot detection, geographic inference, and audience-measurement methodologies.

Advertising attribution and conversion measurement should be independently validated before being used for financial or contractual decisions.

Self-hosted open-source analytics deployments require appropriate privacy controls, data governance, security, backups, access controls, and compliance with applicable privacy regulations.

Many open-source projects listed here provide analytics infrastructure, data pipelines, dashboards, hosting capabilities, or measurement components rather than complete turnkey replacements for commercial podcast analytics platforms.
